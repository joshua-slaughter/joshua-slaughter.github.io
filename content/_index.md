I am a Ph.D. candidate at the University of Edinburgh’s School of Informatics with the support of the Marshall Scholarship, where I am advised jointly by Ava Khamseh and Chris Ponting. Here, I work on projects that use responsible machine learning frameworks to quantify the effects of genetics and socioecological factors on human health and disease. My research focuses on complex-trait genetics, machine learning, and semiparametric statistics. In this work, I contributed to [TMLE.jl](https://github.com/TARGENE/TMLE.jl) to enable fast semiparametric estimation from tabular data for case-control study designs and developed [TarGene](https://github.com/TARGENE/targene-pipeline), a reproducible pipeline for the semiparametric estimation of genetic effects on complex human traits.

Together, these efforts have enabled semiparametric approaches for Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS), which I am currently using to investigate genetic contributions to the risk of Myalgic Encephalomyelitis/Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (ME/CFS). I have applied these methods to [DecodeME](https://www.decodeme.org.uk/), the largest modern ME/CFS study to date, and to researching ME/CFS risk in biobank contexts, where I found the first replicated genomic associations with ME/CFS risk.

Outside of my Ph.D. work, I engage with public health and health services research and advocacy. Here, I have had the great opportunity to work with non-profits, legislators, and public health experts to conduct research towards identifying key levers in providing children in Virginia with accessible and comprehensive health care.
